Why Gilsung asks for gender once, and what it never asks

Published 10 August 2026 by Pacefyx, Inc. We make Gilsung, so this is a page about our own app.

Gilsung asks for gender once, because the luck-pillar direction depends on it. It is a toggle in your details, and you can change it whenever you want.

That is the whole of it. The luck pillars are the only part of Four Pillars that reads the answer, and Gilsung asks for nothing else about you.

What the luck pillars actually need it for

In Four Pillars, the luck pillars run forward or backward depending on the polarity of your year stem together with gender. Yang stem one way, yin stem the other. It is arithmetic on the sexagenary cycle, it is how the method has always been built, and every Four Pillars calculator does the same thing with it.

So the field in Gilsung is named for its job. It reads Gender (for luck-pillar direction), with the reason printed under it: the luck pillars run forward or backward depending on the stem polarity and gender. A question that shows you what it is for is a different question from one that does not.

Where it lives

It sits in your birth details, next to your date, time and place, as a toggle. You can open your details and change it at any point, without starting over and without losing the charts and people you have saved. If you change it, the luck-pillar sequence redraws and that is the only thing that moves.

Nothing else in Gilsung reads it

Your day master, your ten gods, your elements, your hidden stems, your compatibility readings and every line of every reading come out of the eight characters. The toggle touches the direction of the luck pillars, and there it stops.

It is not a demographic field, and it is not a profile. Gilsung has no account and no server to hold a profile on, so there is nothing for the answer to become.

What Gilsung's readings never say

No reading in Gilsung tells you what a woman's chart means, or what a man should do about it. Not once, in any of the seven languages.

Four Pillars is old, and a great deal of what was written around it assumed that a woman's chart was to be read for her marriage and a man's for his career. That is the era, not the method. Gilsung keeps the method, in full and without softening it: the polarity, the day master, the ten gods, the clashes, the seasons, the demanding luck pillars named as demanding. The advice that came wrapped around them is not in the app.

Keep the technique. Leave the century it was written in.

Relationships are named by role

You say who someone is to you, and the choices are partner, parent, child, friend, colleague and frenemy. None of those roles has a gender attached to it, and Gilsung never asks who plays which part.

Two charts are two charts. Gilsung reads any pair the same way, and what changes the reading is the kind of relationship you said it was, which is the thing that actually changes what is worth saying.

Who finds out who you are reading about

Nobody, because Gilsung never receives it. Your birth details, the people you add, the toggle and every reading are worked out on your phone and stay there. There is no account to make and the App Store privacy label for Gilsung reads Data Not Collected.

Compatibility is the sharpest version of this problem, and it is worth being plain about why. To read two charts together, an app has to know that you are reading about that person. Held on a server, that is a record of who you are seeing, sitting in a database for as long as the company lasts. Worked out on your phone, it is not a record of anything.

A field that does not exist cannot leak, cannot be subpoenaed, and cannot be sold with the company. Companies get bought. Nobody can buy what we never had, and that is a stronger protection than any promise we could make about how carefully we would have looked after it.

Is Gilsung in your language?

Gilsung is written in seven languages: English, Indonesian, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese. Five of them are in a script that is not the Latin alphabet.

Your chart is read in your language, in your own script.

In a Four Pillars app the words are the substance, so each language uses the tradition's own vocabulary rather than one romanisation handed to everybody. The day master is 일간 in Korean and 日干 in Japanese, because those are the words those readers grew up with. Every language was written by someone working in it, against a glossary, and read back by a second independent reviewer.

The half that usually breaks is the half you type into. A Buddhist-era year, a decimal comma, digits in your own numerals: get those wrong and the app hands you a chart where every visible word is correct and the birth hour is not, which in Four Pillars changes a whole pillar. Gilsung localises the input as carefully as the output.

Are more languages coming?

Yes. We are still adding languages, and one gets added when it can be done properly, by someone working in it. That is the only gate, and it is the reason the list grows slowly.

Why Gilsung was built this way

Gilsung was thought up in the Bay Area, where you assume as a matter of course that the person next to you might be anyone, and that a form which demands they pick a side is a badly designed form. So the questions never got asked in the first place, and the one that survived only survived because the arithmetic genuinely needs it.

That is the line we drew, and it is worth saying out loud. A tradition gets to keep the parts that do the work. It does not get to keep the parts that are only about who was allowed to be what. Everything Gilsung inherited went through that question, and the answer is a one-line toggle with its purpose printed underneath.

If you have ever opened a saju app and been asked things it had no reason to want: this one asks once, tells you why, and lets you change it.

Common questions

Yes, whenever you want. It is a toggle in your details, and you can edit it without starting over or losing anything you have saved.

Yes, exactly as it works for anyone else. Gilsung reads two charts against each other and never asks which of you is which.
